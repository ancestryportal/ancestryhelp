==========================================================
How to Get Ancestry Help
==========================================================
Whether you are just beginning your family history journey or are an experienced researcher, there may be times when you need help using Ancestry.

From understanding how to search records and interpret DNA matches to resolving account issues and finding professional research assistance, Ancestry provides multiple support channels to assist you. This comprehensive guide covers all the ways you can get Ancestry help, including self-service resources, community collaboration tools, professional genealogy services, and direct customer support contact information.

Ancestry Help Self-Service Resources
====================================

The Ancestry Support Center is the best place to start when you need help [citation:2]. It contains answers to common questions, step-by-step instructions, and tips for family history research.

To open the Support Center, click the circled question mark in the top-right corner of any Ancestry page and select Support Center. Once there, type your question into the search bar to quickly find the help you need [citation:2].

The Support Center covers a wide range of topics, including:

Account Management
------------------

If you need help with signing in, resetting your password, or managing your account settings, the Support Center provides detailed instructions. If you cannot find your family tree, the Support Center explains how to locate it by checking if you are signed in to the correct account [citation:10]. Ancestry does not delete trees, so if you created a tree and did not delete it yourself, it is still there [citation:10].

DNA Kit Registration and Results
--------------------------------

If you have received AncestryDNA as a gift, the recipient will need to create their own free Ancestry account to register the kit [citation:4]. Each adult who takes an AncestryDNA test must have their own account, and only one DNA kit can be registered per account, except for minor children [citation:4]. No membership is required to view DNA results, including ancestral origins, DNA matches, and some traits [citation:4]. However, a membership can help you make more discoveries [citation:4].

Finding Records
---------------

With a membership, you gain access to over 30 billion records from more than 80 countries, including census, birth, marriage, death, and immigration records [citation:1]. You can also see over 100 million public family trees created by other members [citation:1]. To search for records, click the Search tab and select All Collections. Enter information to search with, and use the "Show more options" link to refine your search by location, collection focus, and other criteria [citation:1].

Ancestry Hints are records that may mention the people in your tree. When you see a leaf appear on a person in your tree, click it to review the hint. You can save the record to your tree if it matches, or ignore it if it does not [citation:1].

Collaboration and Community Help
================================

Ancestry provides several tools for connecting with other researchers who may be able to help you [citation:6].

Messages
--------

You can send and receive messages with anyone on Ancestry. To access your messages, click the envelope icon in the top-right corner of any page and select "See all messages" [citation:6].

Message Boards
--------------

Ancestry message boards allow you to post, search for, and read messages about specific topics. These boards are useful for connecting with people who have similar research interests. You can search the message boards for names, locations, historical events, and any other keywords [citation:6].

Member Connect
--------------

Member Connect shows you what other people on Ancestry are researching that relates to your research. You can receive updates when information is added [citation:6].

Member Search
-------------

Use Member Search to find other Ancestry members who are researching surnames, locations, and years of interest to you [citation:6].

Professional Genealogy Services
===============================

If you need more personalized help with your family history research, Ancestry offers professional services through AncestryProGenealogists and one-on-one coaching sessions [citation:2].

AncestryProGenealogists
-----------------------

AncestryProGenealogists have years of experience helping clients tackle difficult family history problems, find family members through DNA analysis, and trace their family trees [citation:2]. Using online and offline resources and experts around the world, professional genealogists can help you reach your family history goals. Depending on the complexity of your research goals, pricing may start as low as $3,700 USD [citation:2].

One-on-One Coaching
-------------------

You can connect one-on-one with a professional genealogist in a private, virtual coaching consultation. In a 30-minute private session, you will get personalized guidance to help with your specific research questions [citation:2].

AncestryDNA Help
================

If you have taken an AncestryDNA test and need help understanding your results, the Support Center provides detailed articles on viewing DNA matches, understanding ethnicity estimates, and interpreting traits [citation:5].

DNA matches are other AncestryDNA members who share DNA with you. Your matches are based only on your DNA—Ancestry does not take your family trees into account [citation:5]. You can message your matches, write notes about each one, edit your relationships, organize them with groups and filters, and see if they have a family tree [citation:5].

If matches are turned off, you will not be able to see or be seen by matches. If two-step verification is off and you sign in with your username or email instead of Apple or Google, you will not be able to access your matches [citation:5].

To see your matches’ full trees or use features that rely on access to trees, such as matching last names and birthplaces, common ancestors, and ThruLines, you will need an Ancestry family history membership [citation:5].

Getting help with Ancestry is straightforward when you know where to look.
